APAC Supplier Spend & Invoice Analysis

Analyzed 2,700 suppliers across 6 APAC countries, processing 70,570+ invoices and $562M+ in total spend to determine exactly which suppliers were worth the effort of onboarding to the SAP Business Network — and which were not.

Impact: Narrowed the client’s focus from 2,700 suppliers down to a prioritized subset that drives real ROI. By applying match-confidence scoring and invoice thresholds, proved that “High” confidence suppliers with >10 invoices capture 84.74% of their segment’s invoices and “Medium” confidence suppliers capture 94.69% — demonstrating that a focused conversion effort on a small group delivers outsized returns. This saves the client months of manual email-based invoice processing and focuses SBN onboarding effort where it actually matters.

  • Match-confidence breakdown (2,700 suppliers total):
    • High confidence: 164 suppliers (5.8%) — 3.87% of spend, 4.56% of invoices
    • Medium confidence: 296 suppliers (10.55%) — 37.32% of total spend, 15.38% of invoices
    • Not Sent For Match: 987 suppliers (37.29%) — 17.88% of spend, 9.02% of invoices
    • Blank / unmatched: 1,253 suppliers (46.36%) — 40.94% of spend, 71.03% of invoices
  • Key finding: Medium-confidence suppliers punch far above their weight — only 10.55% of the supplier base but 37.32% of total spend. These are the #1 conversion targets for SBN onboarding.
  • Invoice capture rates (filtering to suppliers with >10 invoices):
    • High confidence: 1,949 ÷ 2,300 = 84.74% of invoices; $1.8M ÷ $3.4M = 53.80% of spend
    • Medium confidence: 7,350 ÷ 7,762 = 94.69% of invoices; $3.1M ÷ $4.1M = 74.62% of spend
    • Blank / unmatched: 33,709 ÷ 35,389 = 95.25% of invoices; $7.9M ÷ $9.8M = 80.50% of spend
  • SBN status: 460 suppliers already matched on SBN · 1,713 submitted for matching — leaving a clear gap of unmatched suppliers still processing all invoices manually via email
  • Eliminated wasted effort: instead of chasing all 2,700 suppliers with emails, calls, and onboarding paperwork, the team now focuses on the subset that actually drives ROI — saving months of outreach time
  • Long-term value: the scoping logic, confidence scoring, and ranked output became a reusable template — applicable to every future wave/region the client onboards, not just APAC
  • Supported adoption: partnered with Kristi Choate (Partner, SAP Process Adoption) to align the analysis to real change-management needs
SAP · Spend Analysis · Client Delivery · Strategic Scoping

ULINE UNSPSC Code Remediation & Catalog Taxonomy

Analyzed 1,002 UNSPSC codes across the ULINE catalog (US.MX Spring 2026) to ensure every catalog item was mapped to the correct custom commodity code — fixing months of incorrect tax classification and AP routing that had been running since August 2025.

Impact: Before this analysis, a single UNSPSC code was assigned to all catalog items — meaning every item was taxed and audited identically regardless of what it actually was. From August 2025 through July 2026, that’s months of incorrect paper GL account associations, wrong tax treatment, and misrouted accounts payable. After: each item carries the proper custom commodity code, driving correct taxation, accurate AP posting, and audit-ready categorization going forward.

  • Scale: analyzed 1,002 unique UNSPSC codes, mapped against 300 custom commodity codes and 1,670 UNSPSC-to-Custom mappings — part of a broader 9-supplier, 123,830-row analysis matrix
  • Accuracy achieved: 843 codes (84%) received exact client/workbook mappings on first pass; 141 additional codes resolved at family-level; only 18 true gaps remained with no custom-code coverage
  • Corrected systemic error: identified and remediated the one-code-for-all problem that had been misclassifying items for ~11 months — every order placed through ULINE on the SBN during that window was taxed incorrectly
  • Long-term value: the mapping matrix is reusable for every future catalog supplier the client onboards — Bechtle, Amazon, VWR, Hoffman, Sahlberg, RS Germany, and Lyreco (UK & Germany) are already included in the same workbook framework
  • Audit-ready: proper commodity coding ensures GL accounts (taxation) and accounts payable are correctly associated, making the catalog defensible in future audits

"Turning Prior Authorization Delays into Faster Patient Care"

As Solution Demonstration Lead on OMA Team 2 (11 members), I spearheaded the design and build of AskRX — an AI-powered prior authorization platform targeting DaVita Kidney Care. Built from concept to client-ready working demo using ICA Edge as part of IBM's Growth Strategy to Story Challenge.

The Problem: 90% of PA documents require additional clinical information. Physicians like Dr. Mark Murdock lose 12+ hrs/week to paperwork. Manual PA costs $26–$33 per transaction — and patients wait 1–3+ days for care.

Our Solution: A 4-step workflow — Draft, Score, Fix, Submit. AskRX predicts approval likelihood, flags missing fields, recommends edits from previously approved cases, and auto-formats to each payer's template. Unlike competitors (Cohere, Rhyme, Availity) that automate the process, AskRX fixes the input — preventing denials before they happen.

Reflections & Lessons

What This Summer Taught Me

The moments that shaped how I think about consulting, business, and staying steady under pressure.

01

Witnessing a Quarter Close — and keeping your head up

I got a front-row seat to the end-of-quarter rush and IBM's Q2 2025 earnings. IBM beat Wall Street — $17.0B revenue (+8%), EPS of $2.80 vs. $2.65 expected, and raised its full-year outlook — yet the stock still slid on a slight software miss. The lesson stuck with me: the market's first reaction rarely tells the whole story. Real strength — growing margins, a $7.5B GenAI book, disciplined execution — doesn't always show up in the day-one headline. Learning to keep your head up when the surface read looks discouraging is a skill in business and in a consulting career.

02

Selling is a relationship, not a transaction

Shadowing the quarter-end deal rush and talking with Mike Mojica showed me the push-and-pull of closing: a client wants a better price, and IBM weighs what concessions make sense. The best consultants lead clients to the answer — helping them feel that the right idea was theirs. Influence beats insistence.

03

Trust is earned in drips and lost in buckets

Every small, reliable delivery — a clean analysis, a doc that actually helps, showing up prepared — earns a little trust. I learned to protect that compounding by being consistent, asking questions early, and never overpromising. It's the quiet foundation everything else is built on.

04

Gather data points before drawing the map

Sri Narasimhan's advice reframed how I think about my career: at 21, you can't triangulate a trajectory without first collecting a data set.
